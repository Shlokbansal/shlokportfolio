Engineering problem

A hospital laboratory repeatedly received high magnesium quality-control results on an ARCHITECT c4000 analyzer. The issue led to repeat troubleshooting and reduced confidence in the assay’s readiness.

The first task was to determine whether the unexpected result came from operator technique, an assay-related issue, or instrument behavior. Replacing parts before reproducing the symptom would have left the cause uncertain.

My contribution

I owned the service investigation from reproducing the high QC result through isolating the mechanical issue, replacing the affected assembly, verifying instrument diagnostics and QC, and following through with the laboratory.

The key decision was to move beyond static maintenance checks and observe the pipettor under a dynamic range-of-motion diagnostic.

Technical approach

Evidence used to narrow the cause
CheckObservation and decision
Reproduce the resultHigh QC reproduced independently, so operator technique did not explain the failure.

Observe dynamic pipettor motionRange-of-motion testing exposed slow, restricted travel in one reagent pipettor.
Inspect the mechanical driveDisassembly revealed a worn drive belt that limited travel, supporting restricted reagent aspiration as the mechanism.

  • Repair the confirmed mechanism

    Ordered and installed a replacement reagent-pipettor assembly, then completed the required instrument diagnostics.

  • Verify the output that triggered the investigation

    The laboratory reran low, medium, and high magnesium QC. All three returned to their established acceptable ranges.

  • Follow the result over time

    Continued customer support provided follow-up on whether the same failure signature returned.

Result

3 QC levels passed

no recurrence over at least four months of continued support

Low, medium, and high magnesium QC returned to acceptable ranges after the assembly replacement. The original failure did not recur over at least four months of continued customer support.

This was one resolved service event. It did not establish a population-wide reliability improvement or result in a product-design change.

Lessons learned

When static checks pass, observe the mechanism under motion. The discriminating evidence was restricted pipettor travel during a dynamic test, followed by direct inspection of the drive mechanism.

Close the investigation with the original output: a repaired mechanism is only part of the evidence; passing QC and follow-up established whether the corrective action addressed the reported problem. A recurring pattern would justify trending and further engineering review before recommending a maintenance-interval or diagnostic change.
